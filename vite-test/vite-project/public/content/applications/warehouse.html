Why Warehouses Are a Different Job

In a home, bird droppings are unpleasant. In a warehouse they are a contamination finding — on stock, on packaging, on the picking floor. For food, pharma and FMCG operations this turns bird control from a maintenance task into an audit requirement, which changes both the urgency and who signs off on it.

The physical difference is scale and height. Birds roost on purlins, truss chords, beam tops and light fittings, thirty feet up. There is a great deal of linear steel, and every metre of it is a potential perch.

Watch where they enter, not just where they sit

Warehouse birds usually come in through a permanently open shutter, a gable vent or a broken louvre. Treating the roost while leaving the entry open means you are treating symptoms forever.

Spike the Steel or Net the Bay?

What you see What it means Treatment
Droppings in lines under purlins Birds perch on the members Spike the top surface of the members
Nests in the truss apex They are settling inside the bay Net the bay opening
Droppings spread across a whole aisle They are flying through, not perching Close the entry point first
Droppings on stock near a shutter Entry point is the shutter Strip curtain or net at the opening

Most real warehouses need both: netting at the entry points, spikes on the members that remain attractive.

How a Warehouse Is Quantified

  1. Count the member runs. Purlins per bay × bay length × number of bays. This is the bulk of the quantity.
  2. Add the beam tops and any horizontal flange wide enough to stand on.
  3. Measure the openings separately — gable vents, louvres, shutter heads — in square metres for netting.
  4. List the light fittings and cable trays; both are common perches. See cable tray jobs.
  5. Note the access for each zone. Scissor lift, scaffold or rope access changes the programme far more than the material does.

Which Product for a Warehouse

Heavy-duty MS spikes

Powder-coated mild steel for structural members. Deters crows and kites, not just pigeons, and holds up to industrial dust and fumes.

Stainless spikes

For humid, coastal or wash-down environments where mild steel would corrode.

Netting

For bay openings, gable vents and truss voids — anywhere the birds fly through rather than land.

Cable tray treatment

Trays are a continuous perch directly above the floor. Usually spiked along the top edge.

Light fitting caps

High bay fittings are a favourite perch. Treat them at the same time as the members or they become the new roost.

Entry closures

Shutter heads and louvres. Nothing above works permanently until the entry is closed.

Planning the Work

Warehouse bird control is almost always done in a shutdown or a low-activity window, which means the schedule matters as much as the specification.

  1. Survey with the drawings. Bay count, purlin spacing and opening sizes come off the drawings faster than off a ladder.
  2. Agree the zones. Which bays are treated in this window and which in the next, so the order is complete for each phase.
  3. Order the whole schedule. Running short mid-shutdown means the remaining bays wait for the next one.
  4. Clean before fitting and dispose of nesting material properly, particularly in food-grade areas.
  5. Record what was done per bay — auditors ask, and it makes the next phase straightforward.

You get one shutdown

This is the single biggest reason industrial jobs go wrong. Under-ordering by 10% does not delay the job by 10% — it delays the untreated bays until the next shutdown window, which may be months away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I spike the trusses or net the bays?

Spike if the droppings fall in lines under members — that means perching. Net if the birds nest inside the bay or fly through it.

How is a warehouse quantity worked out?

Purlins per bay times bay length times bay count, plus beam tops, cable trays and light fittings. Openings are measured separately in square metres.

Does bird control affect food safety audits?

Yes. Bird activity over stock is a standard finding in food, pharma and FMCG audits, and treatment records are usually requested.

Which material for an industrial roof space?

Powder-coated mild steel for general industrial use; stainless where there is humidity, coastal air or wash-down.

Can this be done without a shutdown?

Sometimes, zone by zone in low-activity periods. It takes longer and needs tighter coordination with operations.

Do you supply to contractors?

What about the birds already inside?

They must be able to leave before openings are closed. Never net a bay with birds or active nests still in it.
