Why Application Matters More Than Product

Most people start by choosing a product. That is backwards. The application decides almost everything: which surfaces need treating, how the quantity is worked out, what the spikes can be fixed to, and whether you can do it yourself or need an access plan.

A balcony job and a warehouse job might use the same steel strip. What differs is that one is fifteen feet of railing you can reach from a chair, and the other is four hundred metres of truss you get one shutdown window to reach. The product is the small decision.

The measurement that matters

Bird control is priced on running length — the total metres of edge that birds land on. Not floor area, not the number of rooms. Walk the edges and add them up, including corners.

Residential Applications

Balcony

The most common job we supply. A typical railing is 10 to 15 ft. Treat the sill above it at the same time or the birds simply move up.

Window Sill

Narrow surfaces, short runs, several of them. A slim base matters here — a wide strip overhangs the sill.

AC Outdoor Unit

Under a metre per unit. Best fitted during a routine service. Use adhesive so the casing is not pierced and keep the vents clear.

Residential jobs are almost always DIY. The kits ship with adhesive, gloves and an illustrated guide in Hindi and English. The hard part is usually society approval, not the fitting.

Commercial and Industrial Applications

Rooftop

Parapet perimeters run long. Measure the full loop before you price anything, and expect multiple rows on wide copings.

Warehouse

Trusses, purlins and beam tops. The decision is whether to spike the members or net the bay — it depends on whether birds land or fly through.

Factory

A schedule of items done inside one shutdown. Procurement needs the full takeoff up front, because there is no second visit.

Cable Tray

Falls between the electrical contractor and the bird control contractor. Getting it scheduled is harder than fitting it.

Solar Panels

Birds nest in the gap under the array. Treat the edge to block access underneath — nothing goes on the panel face.

Something else?

Signage, atriums, covered walkways, temple structures. Send photographs and we will tell you how it is measured.

Common Mistakes Across Every Application

  1. Treating one surface at a time. Birds move to the nearest untreated edge. Spike the railing and leave the sill open and you have moved the problem eighteen inches.
  2. Measuring area instead of edge. Bird control is a linear quantity. Floor area tells you nothing about what you need.
  3. Forgetting corners. The last few inches of every run are where people stop — and exactly where birds land. Add about 10%.
  4. Fitting to a dirty surface. Adhesive will not bond to droppings or flaking paint, and birds navigate back to the smell of the old nest.
  5. Removing it too early. Established birds test a treated spot for two to three weeks before giving up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know which application page applies to me?

Pick the structure the birds are actually using. If they sit on your balcony railing, that is the balcony job — even if the building is commercial.

How is bird control priced?

On running length: the total metres of edge to be treated, plus adhesive or fixings. Access adds cost on high or awkward sites.

Can I do it myself?

Residential jobs, yes — balconies, sills and AC units are all DIY with a kit. Industrial work at height needs an access plan and usually a contractor.

Do you supply to contractors?

Yes. We supply direct at project rates and can provide the takeoff against your drawings.

What do you need from me to quote?

A photograph with something for scale, the running length in feet or metres, and a note on whether the birds are landing on the surface or flying past it.
