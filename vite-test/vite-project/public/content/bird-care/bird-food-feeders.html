What to Fill It With

Indian garden birds are mostly small seed-eaters, and the things they actually want are ordinary and cheap — available in any kirana shop or grain market.

GrainSuitsNotes
Bajra (pearl millet)Sparrows, munias, silverbills, dovesThe reliable default. Cheap, widely available, taken by almost everything small.
Kangni (foxtail millet)Munias, silverbills, small finchesSmall grain, popular with the smallest birds.
Jowar (sorghum)Doves, larger sparrowsLarger grain; mix rather than use alone.
Broken riceSparrows, doves, muniasRaw and broken, never cooked.
Sunflower seedsParakeets, bulbuls, mynasHigh energy, good in winter. Plain and unsalted only.
Plain unsalted peanuts, crushedMynas, bulbuls, parakeetsCrushed, not whole. Never salted or masala-coated.

A simple bajra-and-kangni mix, with sunflower added in winter, covers almost every garden bird you are likely to attract. There is no need for imported seed mixes.

What Never to Put Out

Bread, roti, biscuitsClose to no usable nutrition for a bird, and it fills them up so they stop looking for real food. Chicks raised on it do badly. This is the most-fed harmful item in India.

Mouldy or musty grainSee the section below — this one actually kills.

If in doubt, the test is simple: would this grain have existed in a field? Bajra yes, biscuit no.

The Monsoon Problem: Damp Grain Grows Fungus

The one way a well-meant feeder does real harm

Grain that gets damp and stays damp grows mould — Aspergillus in particular. It produces toxins, and it also causes a respiratory infection in birds that is frequently fatal. A feeder left full through a humid Indian monsoon can become a genuinely dangerous object.

The warning signs are easy to miss because you are looking at the outside of a clear tube: caked grain that has stopped flowing to the ports, a musty smell when you open it, any grey, green or white growth, or a solid plug at the bottom that never empties.

What prevents it:

  • Small fills in monsoon. Enough for two or three days, not two weeks.
  • Hang it with overhead cover — under a branch or an eave.
  • Empty it completely before refilling. Never top up on top of old grain; the oldest sits at the bottom where you cannot see it.
  • Wash and dry fully between fills in humid weather. Dry matters as much as wash.
  • Clear the tray each time. Wet grain sits there first.
  • Throw out anything that smells musty. Do not give it a chance.

The same applies to how you store the sack at home — grain that arrives damp is damp in the feeder too.

Nesting seasonKeep it going, but understand its limits — see below.

One honest limitation: most small birds feed their chicks on insects, not grain. A feeder supports the adults; it does not raise the young. If you want to help with that, the useful things are not buying anything — not spraying insecticide, leaving some unswept leaf litter, and keeping a few flowering plants.

Refilling Well

  1. Empty what is left before adding new grain.
  2. Check the bottom of the tube — caking starts where you cannot see it.
  3. Rinse and dry the tray, every time.
  4. Fill to the amount that gets eaten in a few days, not to the top out of habit.
  5. Full clean periodically — dismantle, wash, dry completely.
  6. Keep it consistent. Birds build a feeder into a daily route; in a cold spell, that reliability is the value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best grain for Indian garden birds?

Bajra is the reliable default — cheap, everywhere, and taken by sparrows, munias, silverbills and doves alike. Add kangni for the smallest birds and sunflower seeds in winter.

Can I feed bread to birds?

No. It has close to no usable nutrition for them and it fills them up so they stop foraging for real food. It is the most commonly fed harmful item in India, and it is always fed with good intentions.

Is cooked rice all right?

Raw broken rice is fine. Cooked rice with salt or oil is not, and it spoils within hours in Indian heat.

How much should I put in at a time?

However much gets eaten in a few days — less in monsoon. A full tube that sits for two weeks in humid weather is a fungus risk, not generosity.

The grain has caked and stopped coming down.

Damp has got in. Empty it completely, wash it, dry it fully before refilling, and throw the old grain away rather than reusing it — especially if it smells musty.

Will this bring pigeons?

No. The ports and perches are sized for small birds, so pigeons cannot use them. What brings pigeons is grain scattered loose on a terrace or balcony.

Do I need to feed all year?

You do not have to, but consistency helps once birds have built it into their routine. If you are going to take a break, summer is the time — put water out instead.

Feeding Well Rather Than Just Feeding

A feeder, plain bajra, small fills and a clean tray. That is the whole method, and it outperforms anything more elaborate.
