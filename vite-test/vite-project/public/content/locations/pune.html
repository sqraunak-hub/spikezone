The Argument for Doing It at Construction Stage

Same work, a fraction of the price

On a finished building, most of what you pay for bird control above ground level is access — the cradle, the tower, the rope team, the society approvals, the resident notices. The material is the small part.

During construction, all of that already exists. The scaffolding is up, the site has access equipment, and there is no committee to persuade. Fitting parapet runs, sill details and shaft netting at that point is close to marginal cost.

It also avoids the thing that actually happens otherwise: a new Pune tower is handed over clean, pigeons colonise the parapets and shafts within the first two seasons, and the society then pays full retrofit price for work that could have been done for a fraction before handover.

If you are a builder, a project manager or a facilities team taking over a new building, this is the single most useful thing on this page.

The surfaces worth including at that stage are the ones that are expensive to reach later: parapets, roof edges, service shaft openings, facade bands and podium canopies. Balconies can be retrofitted more easily, so they are a lower priority at that stage.

Pune's monsoon is long rather than merely heavy, which is what narrows the window. The practical consequence for a society: decide in the monsoon, fit in October. Decide in August and the good fitters are already booked.

No Salt Changes the Fixing Advice

Worth stating plainly because Mumbai advice gets applied to Pune by default and it is unnecessarily conservative here.

  • Pune is inland. There is no salt-laden air, so the corrosion problem that dominates coastal specification does not apply.
  • Adhesive fixing is viable on clean, sound substrate at reachable heights — which makes small jobs cheaper and self-fitting realistic.
  • Mechanical fixing is still right at height, on exposed edges, and anywhere above a walkway or entrance.
  • Moderate temperature range means less thermal cycling stress on fixings than Delhi or Ahmedabad.
  • UV still matters. Milder does not mean weak sun — unstabilised plastic still fails.

Frequently Asked Questions

We are building a project. Should this be in the scope?

Yes, and it is the cheapest possible moment. The scaffolding is up, the access is paid for and there is no committee to persuade. Retrofitting the same parapets after handover costs several times as much in access alone.

When should a finished building be done?

October to May. Pune's monsoon is long as well as heavy, and nothing bonds to wet masonry. Decide before the rains if you want to be fitted in October.

Do we need the coastal specification like Mumbai?

No. Pune is inland with no salt-laden air, so adhesive fixing is viable on clean substrate at reachable heights. Mechanical fixing is still the right call at height and on exposed edges.

Can I fit it myself on my flat?

Spikes on a reachable sill, railing or AC unit, yes. Netting a balcony is worth having done — tension is what makes a net work, and a slack net is worse than none.

Do you supply Chakan and Pimpri?

Yes — girders, trusses, cable trays and warehouse netting, quoted as a schedule of items against your shutdown dates.

Our society is deciding at the AGM.

Ask us for the per-flat figure at society quantity as well as the total. That comparison is usually what carries the vote.

Building Now, or Fixing It Later?

If the scaffolding is still up, tell us — that is the cheapest bird control you will ever buy for that building. If it is a finished society, send one balcony opening size and the flat count, and aim for an October start.
