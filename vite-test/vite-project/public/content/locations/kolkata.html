At a Glance

  • Cornices, not parapets. Kolkata's colonial stock is full of deep projecting bands — sheltered on two sides, which makes them nesting sites rather than perches.
  • The row goes against the wall on a cornice, not along the outer edge. That is the opposite of every other surface, and it is the commonest mistake here.
  • Humidity is the failure mode, not ultraviolet. Damp attacks adhesive fixings and anything sealed into a cavity.
  • Crows matter here as well as pigeons — heavier, bolder, and they use higher and more open perches.
  • Fit between November and February. That is the dry, stable window.

Why the Building Stock Changes the Job

Most Indian cities are a bird control problem of flat parapets and balcony openings. Kolkata is not. Its older buildings — the North Kolkata houses, the Dalhousie and Park Street blocks, the institutional buildings — carry deep decorative cornices, heavy string courses, boxed shuttered window heads and generous chajjas.

Every one of those is a corner rather than a shelf: a horizontal surface with a wall behind it and an overhang above. Deep, dry and sheltered on two sides is a nest site, not a perch — which means a resident pair returning for years rather than passing visitors, and it changes both the urgency and the method.

Where the row actually goes

On a plain ledge or parapet you set the row toward the outer edge, because that is where birds land. On a Kolkata cornice they land there and then walk back into the corner, because the corner is the sheltered part.

A row fitted along the outer edge alone leaves the good part open and the birds simply step over it. The row belongs hard against the wall junction, with a second toward the outer edge on anything deeper than about 200 mm. Detail on cornices and beam junctions.

The lime plaster point is worth taking seriously on older buildings. Nesting material packed into a decorative profile holds moisture against the wall, and in Kolkata's humidity that causes spalling and loosens the moulding from the structure. On buildings of that age, pieces do eventually come down. Fitting a deterrent to already-failing plaster attaches it to material that is on its way off the wall — repair first.

Crows as Well as Pigeons

Kolkata is one of the few Indian cities where a bird control brief has to account for crows seriously, not just pigeons. They behave differently and it affects the specification:

  • Heavier and bolder. They will stand over a shallow row that would stop a pigeon, so pin height matters.
  • They prefer open, high perches — roof edges, poles, antennas, overhead cables — rather than sheltered ledges.
  • They investigate things. Loose ends and marginal fixings get pulled at in a way pigeons never manage.
  • They are not deterred by presence. Nothing based on scaring them lasts more than a few days.

Practically: use the wider, taller profile on open high runs, and fix mechanically anywhere crows are the main species.

Frequently Asked Questions

We fitted spikes on our cornice and birds still nest there.

Almost certainly because the row is on the outer edge and they are stepping over it into the sheltered corner behind. On a cornice the row belongs hard against the wall junction.

When should the work be done in Kolkata?

November to February. Humidity and monsoon between them mean nothing bonds reliably for much of the year, and cavities should never be closed while they are damp.

Our building is old and the plaster is crumbling.

Repair first. Nesting material holds water against the wall and is part of why it is spalling, and anything fixed to failing plaster is attached to material that is already coming off.

Crows, not pigeons. Does that change anything?

Yes. Crows are heavier and will stand over a shallow row, so use the taller, wider profile on open runs. They also pull at loose ends, so fix mechanically.

Will it be visible on a heritage facade?

Barely. The polycarbonate is transparent, so there is no colour to sit against the plaster, and set back from the front edge it is not picked out from street level.

Something is nesting inside our boxed window heads.

That is a cavity rather than a surface. Spikes on the outside will not help — the opening has to be closed, and not while there are chicks inside.

Do you deliver to Howrah and the industrial belt?

Yes, including Dankuni, Taratala and Kalyani for warehouse and truss work.
