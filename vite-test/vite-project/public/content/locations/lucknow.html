Lime Plaster Fails Differently

The damage is structural before it is cosmetic

On carved sandstone, droppings etch the surface and detail erodes. On lime plaster and stucco — the finish across most of Lucknow's older stock, from the Imambaras to the ordinary older housing — the mechanism is different and faster.

Nesting material packed into a moulding, a cornice or a bracket holds water against the substrate. Lime plaster is porous and moves with moisture, so sustained damp causes it to blow: the plaster separates from the backing, hollows form, and eventually sections come away. On a building of that age, pieces do come down.

Which produces an unusual sequence on this city's heritage stock:

  1. Clear the material off — this alone stops the damage progressing, and it is the most valuable single action.
  2. Check for chicks first. Most wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.
  3. Assess the plaster. Tap it. Hollow-sounding areas are already detached.
  4. Repair before protecting. Fitting a deterrent to blown plaster attaches it to material on its way off the wall.
  5. Clean gently. Never pressure wash stucco — it takes off more than the droppings did and drives water into the fabric.
  6. Then fit, set back and reversibly.

The Arcades Are a Different Job

Hazratganj and the older commercial streets have deep covered arcades and colonnaded frontages, and they behave nothing like an open facade.

  • The birds are overhead, in the beam-to-ceiling junctions, not on a ledge you can see from the pavement.
  • Those junctions are corners — sheltered on two sides and dry, which makes them nesting sites. See cornices and beam junctions.
  • Netting an open arcade does not work. It is a public walkway with openings at both ends, not an enclosable space.
  • The mess lands on customers, which is why shopkeepers rather than owners usually raise it.
  • Include the column capitals — they are part of the same problem and are almost always left out.

Working over a live shopping street also means the practical constraints of a commercial job: protection below, work outside trading hours, and nothing that can fall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Our plaster is bulging where birds have been nesting.

That is spalling, and it is the characteristic failure here. Nesting material has been holding water against the wall and the lime plaster has separated from its backing. Clear it, let the wall dry, repair, and only then fit.

Can we just fit spikes over it?

Not onto blown plaster. You would be fixing to material that is already coming off the wall, and on an old building the piece that eventually falls takes the spikes with it.

How should the stucco be cleaned?

Gently, and never by pressure washing — it takes off more material than the droppings did and drives water into the fabric. Take conservation advice on the method for protected buildings.

Birds are nesting above our shop in the arcade.

That will be the beam-to-ceiling junction. It is a sheltered corner, so it is a nesting site, and the row goes against the wall junction rather than along the open edge. Netting an open arcade does not work.

Will it be visible on an ornate facade?

Barely. The polycarbonate is transparent, so there is no colour to sit against the stucco, and set back from the front edge it is not picked out from the street.

Our building is a protected monument.

Then written consent comes before the survey. It usually results in a better specification anyway, because approval tends to require a reversible fixing.

When should the work be done?

October to May for fitting. But clear accumulated nesting material before the monsoon rather than after — a wet season with material packed into a moulding is what causes the plaster to blow.

Clear It Before the Rains

Send a close-up of the moulding and a shot from street level, with the run in metres. If the plaster sounds hollow when you tap it, say so — on a Lucknow building that changes the order of work, and it is the difference between a repair and a rebuild.
