Height Changes the Economics

On a Gurgaon tower, the product is the cheap part

A parapet run or a facade band on the eighteenth floor needs a cradle, a rope access team or a tower crane. That access is the bulk of what you pay, and it is paid again in full the second time you go up.

Two practical consequences, and they are the opposite of how people instinctively buy:

  • Be generous when quantifying. Corners, returns, the stretch by the drainpipe, the elevation that is only slightly affected — the marginal metre is effectively free once you are up there.
  • Do adjacent surfaces at the same time. Parapet, roof edge, terrace plant, water tanks and any solar array in one visit rather than four.

This is also why rooftop work is unusually good value even on a tall Gurgaon tower — you reach the roof by lift, not by cradle.

The RWA Decision

Almost every Gurgaon residential job is a condominium job, and the external face of the building is common property. That means an individual flat owner generally cannot fit balcony netting on their own, and the ones who try usually end up removing it.

Peafowl Are a Different Brief

Sectors bordering the Aravallis

Several Gurgaon sectors border green belt and Aravalli land, and peafowl are commonly present there. They are India's national bird and fully protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 — the protection is stronger than for the birds discussed elsewhere on this site, and the consequences of harming one are serious.

They also behave nothing like pigeons. They are large, heavy, ground-dwelling for most of the day, and they roost high at night — on boundary walls, terraces, car porch roofs and parapets. Bird spikes are not sized for that weight.

The workable approach is exclusion from specific surfaces — a boundary wall coping, a porch roof, a terrace parapet they use as a roost — and removing what attracts them, usually food and open water. Tell us if peafowl rather than pigeons are the issue, because the specification is different and the wrong product simply will not stop them.

What Gurgaon Does to an Installation

  • Construction dust is heavier here than almost anywhere — and dust is an adhesion problem. Clean and dry properly or the base bonds to the deposit rather than the wall.
  • 45°C summers and intense UV destroy unstabilised plastic. Ours is UV stabilised; check that whoever you buy from.
  • Wind at height. Above a few floors, mechanical fixing rather than adhesive alone — anything that lets go falls into a podium or a road.
  • Rooftop water tanks everywhere. These are the priority item on most Gurgaon terraces, for obvious reasons.
  • Fit before monsoon, while substrates are dry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I net just my own balcony?

Usually not without RWA permission — the external face is common property in a condominium. Ask first; fitting and then being told to remove it is the expensive route.

Why is a tower job priced the way it is?

Because access dominates. The cradle or rope access costs more than the material, which is why doing every elevation in one visit is far cheaper than returning for the bits you left.

What will convince our RWA?

Not the birds — the droppings. The untreated floors are fouling the balconies below them, which makes it a building problem rather than a few residents' preference.

We have peafowl, not pigeons.

Then the specification changes. Peafowl are large, heavy and fully protected as India's national bird. Bird spikes are not sized for them; the approach is excluding them from specific roosting surfaces and removing food and open water.

Do you deliver to Gurgaon quickly?

Yes — our works are in Delhi, so it is effectively local supply. Collection is also straightforward.

Do you supply Manesar and IMT?

Yes, for warehouse netting, trusses and cable trays.

Our tower has rooftop solar.

Include it while the access is arranged. The gap under an array is the best nesting cavity on any roof and it costs you generation.

Quote the Whole Tower, Not One Floor

Send the opening sizes, the number of flats and the elevation count. If it is going to an RWA meeting, ask us for the per-flat figure at tower quantities as well — that is usually the number that gets it approved.
