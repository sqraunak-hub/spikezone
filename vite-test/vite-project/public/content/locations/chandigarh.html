Appearance Is a Rule, Not a Preference

Ask before you fix anything visible

Chandigarh was designed as a coherent whole, and its planned character is maintained through controls on what may be attached to or altered on the outside of buildings. That is unusual in India, and it changes this purchase.

In practice it means the products that get fitted casually elsewhere — wide opaque spike rows along a parapet front, visible netting across a veranda, anything that reads as an addition from the street — are the wrong starting point here.

What works instead:

  • Transparent polycarbonate, which has no colour to read against the concrete and is not picked out from the road.
  • Set back from the front edge of any parapet, ledge or fin, so nothing breaks the line of the building.
  • Reversible fixing — it lifts off and re-fixes in about a minute, leaving no mark. That is the single most useful sentence to be able to say to an Estate Office.
  • Ask first. Confirm the position for your sector and building type before ordering, not after fitting.

On the Capitol Complex and other Corbusier-designed buildings the position is stricter again — those carry heritage protection and require written consent for anything attached.

The Surfaces This City Actually Has

Chandigarh's architecture produces a distinctive set of bird surfaces, and they are mostly sheltered ones rather than open ledges:

  • Deep verandas and covered walkways — beam-to-ceiling junctions sheltered on two sides, which makes them nesting sites rather than perches. See cornices and beam junctions.
  • Concrete brise-soleil and jaali screens — dozens of small recesses on a single elevation, and the hardest surface here to treat neatly.
  • Projecting sunshades and chajjas over windows, which are usually the actual perch while the sill below just catches what falls.
  • Flat roof parapets on the low-rise sector housing.
  • Porches and car porches, which are open-sided and cannot simply be netted — see porch bird problems.
  • Boundary walls, which double as the monkey route in the northern sectors.

Jaali screens deserve a word of warning. They look like a netting job and usually are not — the recesses are individually small, and closing the whole screen changes the appearance of the elevation, which is exactly what the controls exist to prevent. Send a photograph before assuming either approach.

Monkeys From the Shivalik Side

Chandigarh is the second of only two cities on our list where monkeys are routinely part of the brief. Here the pressure comes from the Shivalik foothills and the green belt rather than from established temple populations.

  • They are protected wildlife. Deterrence is legal; injuring, trapping and relocating are not, and are a Forest Department matter.
  • Boundary walls and parapets are the routes. Block the stretch they actually walk — usually a fraction of the perimeter.
  • It will not make a property monkey-proof. They climb and jump; the honest goal is closing a specific route.
  • Check for an overhanging branch first. In a city this heavily planted, trimming the branch that gives them the jump often does more than any product.
  • Food is the driver — open bins, terrace offerings, feeding.
  • Different product — 2 inch spikes, 4 inch coverage, mechanically fixed. Bird spikes are the wrong scale.
