What the Coast Does to an Installation

This is why metal spikes are the wrong choice in Mumbai

Salt-laden air reaches well inland from the coast, and it attacks metal continuously rather than seasonally. On a bird control installation that shows up in two ways: the pins themselves corrode, and rust runs down the wall below in streaks that are far more visible from the road than the product ever was.

Our spikes are polycarbonate with no metal in them. In most cities that is a durability footnote. In Mumbai it is the specification.

What still needs deciding is the fixing. On a sea-facing or upper-floor elevation, mechanical fixing is the right answer — salt, wind and humidity together are hard on adhesive, and anything that lets go at that height falls into a street.

The Timing Window

Mumbai is the one city on this list where when matters as much as what. The practical calendar:

PeriodSuitabilityWhy
October to MayThe windowDry substrate, stable weather, fixings cure properly.
March to MayBest of allFully dry, and it lands ahead of the monsoon rather than after it.
June to SeptemberAvoid if possibleNothing bonds reliably to wet masonry, and access work at height is unsafe.
Immediately post-monsoonWaitSurfaces look dry and are not. Give walls time.

Societies that decide in July usually end up fitting in October. Deciding in January means the building is protected before the rains, which is also when pigeon nesting pressure is highest.

Why Netting Is the Default Here

In most Indian cities a balcony job is a choice between spikes and net. In Mumbai it is usually net, and the reason is the way flats are actually used.

  • Balconies are working space. Drying, storage, a washing machine, the AC unit — not an ornamental ledge. A balcony in constant use with birds in it is a daily problem, not a cosmetic one.
  • Flats are small, so the balcony is a meaningful share of usable area and losing it to droppings matters more.
  • Towers are tall. Access dominates the cost, which pushes buildings toward doing every flat in one go.
  • Everyone can see the elevation. Flat-by-flat netting in six different styles is exactly what committees object to.
  • Pigeon density is high, so an unnetted balcony between two netted ones is found within days.

Feeding: A Public Question in Mumbai

Kabutarkhanas and pigeon density

Mumbai has long-established designated pigeon-feeding places, and the health effects of concentrated pigeon populations — particularly respiratory complaints associated with dried droppings — have been publicly debated in the city for years.

Whatever position you take on that, the practical point for a building near one is simple: local pigeon density is high and will stay high. Physical exclusion still works exactly as well on the surfaces you protect. What changes is that partial treatment fails faster, because there are always more birds looking for the ledges you left.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do metal bird spikes rust in Mumbai?

Poor-grade metal does, and quickly — salt-laden air works on it all year. The visible result is rust streaking down the wall, which looks worse than the droppings did. Our spikes are polycarbonate with no metal in them.

When should the work be done?

Between October and May, ideally March to May. Nothing bonds reliably to wet masonry, so fitting during the monsoon is the main reason installations fail here.

Adhesive or mechanical fixing?

Mechanical on sea-facing elevations, upper floors and anything over a street. Salt, wind and humidity together are hard on adhesive, and at Mumbai building heights a failure is a safety matter.

Should our society net every balcony?

Usually yes. Access is most of the cost on a tall building, pigeon density is high enough that untreated balconies are found immediately, and a uniform elevation is what committees will actually approve.

Will netting cut the light or the breeze?

Less than people expect, particularly in a larger mesh. From inside you look through it rather than at it. Over-specifying a fine mesh is what costs you light.
