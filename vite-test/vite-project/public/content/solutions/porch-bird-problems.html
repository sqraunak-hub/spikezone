Bird & Pigeon Problems in Porches and Entrances

A porch is the one place where the birds are directly above people rather than beside them. The nest sits in the ceiling corner, and everything that comes off it lands on the doorstep, on visitors, and on whatever is parked underneath.

At a Glance

  • The birds are overhead, in the beam junctions — not on a ledge you can see.
  • Netting an open porch fails. It is an entrance, not an enclosable space.
  • The corner where the beam meets the ceiling is sheltered on two sides, which makes it a nest.
  • It is directly above your front door, so the mess lands on visitors.
  • Include the pillar capitals — they are usually part of the same problem.

Porches get chosen because they are the best shelter on the building: a solid roof overhead, walls on one or two sides, no rain, and almost no wind. To a nesting pair that is close to ideal, and the fact that people walk through twice a day matters less to them than you would expect.

Because the roof is above and the walls are beside, the usable surfaces are not the horizontal bands you find elsewhere. They are the junctions where the ceiling meets a beam or a wall, and anything fixed to the soffit.

Find it by where the droppings pile up

On a porch the droppings do not smear along a line — they collect in one spot on the floor. Stand on that spot and look straight up. Whatever is directly overhead is the perch, and it is usually a beam junction or a light fitting rather than anything you would have guessed.

Why Netting an Open Porch Usually Fails

A porch has no opening to close

Netting works by sealing an opening. A balcony has one. A porch is open on two or three sides by design — that is what makes it a porch. Netting it means enclosing your own entrance, which people almost always dislike once it is up, and which usually needs society or municipal approval on a frontage.

The exception is a deep porch with one recessed void at the back, or a covered car porch where the far end can be closed without affecting the entrance. There, netting that single void is sensible. For the open span, spikes on the soffit junctions are the answer.

What It Actually Costs You

  • It lands on people. Visitors, customers, the person unlocking the door. On a shop or clinic entrance this is the whole problem.
  • Vehicles underneath. Scooters and cars parked in a car porch take it daily.
  • The doorstep needs washing, not sweeping. Dried droppings on tile or stone at an entrance are a slip risk when wet.
  • Mites at the doorway. An old nest directly above a door is the commonest route for bird mites to be noticed indoors.
  • First impression. For a shop, a clinic or a guest house the entrance is the whole frontage, and a fouled porch reads as neglect.
  • Nesting material in the light fitting. Dry material on a warm fitting, right above the door.

Clearing and Protecting a Porch

  1. Check for an active nest first. Most wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972. Porches are used heavily for nesting, so there very often is one. Wait until the young fledge — commonly four to six weeks — then clear and fit the same week.
  2. Stand where the droppings collect and look up. Mark every surface directly overhead.
  3. Isolate the light before touching anything near the fitting.
  4. Clear damp, with the floor covered. Everything falls onto the entrance, so sheet it first.
  5. Spike every junction you marked, not just the one with the nest. A porch has three or four equally good corners and they will simply move.
  6. Deal with the fitting and any disused hook at the same time.
  7. Wash the floor properly once the work is done, rather than sweeping dry.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I stop birds nesting in my porch?

Fit spikes at every beam-to-ceiling junction, on the top of the light fitting and above the door frame. Doing only the corner with the nest in it does not work — a porch has several equally good corners.

Can I just net the porch?

Rarely a good idea. A porch is open by design, so netting it means enclosing your own entrance. It usually looks wrong and often needs approval. Net only a genuine recessed void at the back, if there is one.

How do I find where they are actually sitting?

Stand on the spot where the droppings collect on the floor and look straight up. On a porch the mess lands directly below the perch, so it points at it exactly.

There is a nest above my door with chicks in it.

Leave it until they fledge — four to six weeks is typical, and most wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972. Clear and fit spikes the same week they leave, before the pair returns.

Can spikes go on the light fitting above the door?

Yes, short sections on the top of the housing. Isolate the fitting first, keep clear of ventilation slots, and do not drill a sealed housing.

Is it safe with people walking underneath?

It has to be fixed properly, because this is directly above a doorway. Over an entrance, a mechanical fixing or a mechanical restraint alongside adhesive is the right approach — not adhesive alone.

Do you supply for a row of shops or a society with many entrances?

Yes. Quantity per porch is small, so these are quoted as total running metres across the building.
