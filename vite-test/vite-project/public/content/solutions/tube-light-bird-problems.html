Bird & Pigeon Problems on Tube Lights and Light Fittings

Light fittings get chosen for a reason nothing else on this site shares: they are warm. Birds sit on them in winter for the heat, and the result shows up first as lighting that has quietly got dimmer — usually blamed on the fittings long before anybody looks up and sees why.

At a Glance

  • The warmth of the fitting is the attractant — this is one of the few surfaces birds choose for heat.
  • The consequence is measured in light output: a fouled fitting simply stops delivering its lux.
  • Non-conductive and flame retardant matters here more than anywhere except cable trays.
  • Pieces are usually shorter than one section — it is a per-fitting quantity, ordered by total metres.
  • Twin fittings have a gap between them that is a nest site in its own right.

A surface-mounted batten or a high bay fitting offers a flat top, a bracket to grip, and residual warmth from the driver or ballast. In a cold month that combination is worth more to a bird than shelter, which is why light fittings get colonised in winter even when better-sheltered surfaces are available.

Where the fitting is suspended on a rod or chain, the top of the fitting and the suspension bracket both become perches, and the bird sits directly above the diffuser.

A quick way to check

If someone has said the lighting in a bay "seems dimmer than it used to be", look at the tops of the fittings before ordering lamps. On a fouled diffuser the difference after a clean is immediate and obvious, and it tells you whether you have a lamp problem or a bird problem.

Check what is above the fitting first

Very often the birds are not on the light at all. They are on the purlin or truss above it, and the fitting simply catches what lands. Spiking the fitting in that case changes nothing, because nothing was sitting on it. Look at where the droppings are thickest on the top surface: heavy in one spot means something above, spread evenly means the fitting itself.

Electrical Precautions

  • Isolate before any work. This is work on a live fitting at height, not a masonry job.
  • Nothing that blocks a vent. Fittings shed heat through the housing; do not cover ventilation slots.
  • Nothing conductive bridging terminals. Keep fixings clear of the gear tray and terminal block.
  • Do not drill the housing. It compromises the ingress rating on anything sealed.
  • Keep the diffuser removable. Whatever is fitted must not prevent lamp or driver replacement.
  • Coordinate with maintenance so the work happens on a planned shutdown rather than around live circuits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do birds sit on light fittings?

Warmth, mostly. A fitting holds heat from the driver or ballast, and in winter that is worth more to a bird than shelter. The flat top and the bracket give it somewhere to grip.

Do droppings on light fittings reduce brightness?

Yes, and noticeably. Droppings on a diffuser or a high bay reflector cut output, and because it happens gradually it usually gets blamed on ageing lamps. Clean one fitting and compare before ordering replacements.

Is a nest on a light fitting a fire risk?

Dry nesting material sitting on a fitting that runs warm is combustible material where it should not be. It is normally raised as a safety finding and it is the reason most plants act on it.

Can spikes be fitted on a tube light?

Yes — short sections along the top of the housing and on the suspension bracket. Keep them clear of ventilation slots and the terminal area, and do not drill a sealed housing.

The lights are fouled but I never see birds on them. Why?

Because they are probably on the purlin or truss above, and the fitting is catching what falls. Spiking the light in that case does nothing. Look at where the droppings are heaviest on the top surface to tell which it is.

Should we spike every fitting or net the roof?

If the fittings are the only surface in use, spike them. If the structure above is also being used, netting the void covers the lights, the trays and the floor in one go and usually costs less than doing hundreds of fittings individually.

Do you supply small quantities per fitting?

Yes. These are usually ordered as total running metres across a bay rather than per light, because the section needed for one fitting is short.
