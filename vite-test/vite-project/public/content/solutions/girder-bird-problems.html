Bird & Pigeon Problems on Girders and Steel Beams

Unlike a lattice truss, a girder gives birds something a truss does not: a wide, flat, continuous ledge running the length of the building. That makes it harder to ignore and — usefully — much easier to fix. This is a spike surface, and a straightforward one.

At a Glance

  • The mirror image of a truss: a girder has a flat flange, so spikes are exactly right here.
  • Both flanges or nothing — treat one and they use the other.
  • Crane and gantry travel decides which face can be treated.
  • Long runs, so quantities scale fast — a hall can be several hundred metres.
  • Usually specified alongside the trays and light fittings at the same level.

An I-beam or plate girder has a bottom flange that is typically 100 to 300 mm wide and runs unbroken for the whole span. To a pigeon that is a road: level, wide enough to turn around on, covered by the beam web above and the roof above that. Birds line up along it the same way they line up on a parapet.

The difference from a truss is that there is only one surface per beam, and it is flat. That is why the answer here is spikes and the answer on a lattice truss is netting.

The half-covered flange is the commonest failure

Somebody fits a single narrow row down the centre of a 250 mm flange, and the birds simply stand on the outer edge with their tails over the drop. From the floor it looks like the spikes are not working. They are working, on the 60 mm they cover.

Measure the flange before ordering. On anything over roughly 200 mm, budget for two rows — which means twice the running metres.

Crane and Gantry Girders Need One Extra Check

Clearance to the moving crane

On a gantry or crane girder, anything you fit has to sit entirely outside the clearance envelope of the crane, its trolley and its festoon cabling. A spike strip in that path will be sheared off on the first pass — and dropped onto the floor below.

Before ordering, confirm with your maintenance team which face of the girder is clear through the full travel. Often the inner face is out of bounds and the outer flange is free, which changes where the strips go. This is worth ten minutes and it is the one constraint unique to gantry girders.

What a Girder Roost Causes

  • A continuous line of droppings down the length of the bay, onto whatever the beam spans — machinery, stock, walkways.
  • Fouling on the beam itself, which sits in the flange corner where it stays damp and works on the paint system.
  • Nesting in the web stiffener pockets — the small bays formed between stiffener plates are sheltered on three sides.
  • Material dropping into moving plant where the beam runs above a conveyor or a line.
  • Fouled walkways and a slip risk where a beam crosses over an access route.
  • Inspection access becomes unpleasant, so structural inspections get deferred.

Connections and stiffener pockets are where nests appear first. If the run is long and the budget is finite, protect those before the plain flange.

Fixing to Structural Steel

  • Do not drill or weld structural members. Not without the structural engineer's approval, and generally not at all. Fixing should be adhesive or mechanical clamp only.
  • Galvanised and painted steel needs preparation. Both carry a film that adhesive will not bond through. Clean and degrease, and let it dry fully.
  • Allow for movement. Long steel spans move with temperature. Strips should be fixed as separate lengths with a small gap at the joins rather than as one continuous bonded run.
  • Consider the coating. On a specified paint system, confirm the fixing method will not compromise it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I stop birds sitting on steel beams?

Fit spikes along the bottom flange, and use two rows on anything wider than about 200 mm. Cover the stiffener pockets and connection plates as well — those are where the nests are.

Why do you recommend spikes on a girder but netting on a truss?

Because a girder is one wide flat ledge per beam and a truss is hundreds of angled members. Spikes suit a ledge. Nothing practical suits a lattice at height, so you separate the void instead.

How many rows do I need?

Measure the flange. Up to about 100 mm, one row. Over roughly 200 mm, two rows — otherwise the birds stand on the uncovered edge and it looks as though the spikes have failed.

Can spikes be fitted to a crane gantry girder?

Yes, but only outside the crane's clearance envelope. Confirm with maintenance which face stays clear through the full travel before ordering — anything in the path will be sheared off and dropped.

Will fixing spikes damage the structural steel?

It should not, because nothing is drilled or welded. Adhesive or clamp fixing only, and the surface needs proper preparation on galvanised or painted steel.

The beams are thirty feet up. Is it worth it?

The access is the expensive part, so plan to clean, fix and inspect in one visit rather than three. If several surfaces up there are in use, price netting the bay against spiking the beams before deciding.
