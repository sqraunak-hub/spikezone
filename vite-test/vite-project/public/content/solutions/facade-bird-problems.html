Bird & Pigeon Problems on Building Facades

The streaking down a building face is not coming from the whole facade. It comes from a handful of horizontal bands — sills, string courses, cornices, sunshades — and the rest of the wall is simply where it lands. Identify the bands and the job gets a great deal smaller.

At a Glance

  • Work top down. Birds land on the highest outer line and move inward and downward.
  • The streaking you can see usually starts higher than you think — look a floor or two above it.
  • Access dominates the cost, so sequencing decides the price more than the product does.
  • Do a whole elevation at a time. Treating four floors just concentrates them on the rest.
  • The sheltered corners, not the open bands, are where the nesting happens.

A vertical wall is not usable by birds. Everything that projects from it is. On a typical Indian facade that means the window sills, the sunshades above them, the decorative string courses running horizontally between floors, the cornice at the top, and the parapet above that.

Those bands are a small fraction of the facade area and they generate all of the mess. Because they run the full width of the building, though, the running metres add up quickly — and because they are at height, access rather than material usually sets the price.

Do It Top-Down, in One Visit

The sequencing mistake that wastes the whole job

A facade has to be worked from the top down. Protect the second-floor sills first and the birds carry on using the cornice above, which re-fouls everything you just cleaned. Within a season it looks as though the spikes did nothing.

The same applies to phasing by budget. If only part can be afforded, do the top bands — parapet and cornice — not the ones at eye level. Protecting the highest perch pushes the population off the building; protecting the lowest just moves them up.

Time it with the painting cycle

The single biggest saving available on a facade job is to do it while scaffold or a cradle is already up for painting or repair. The access is the expensive part and it is already paid for. Societies that plan this alongside repainting spend a fraction of what a standalone job costs.

Appearance From Street Level

On a commercial or heritage facade this is a real constraint, and it is worth being accurate about it.

  • Spikes are seen edge-on from the ground. Fitted on a horizontal band and viewed from below, they read as a thin line rather than as a row of pins.
  • The streaking is far more visible than anything fitted to prevent it. Vertical white marks down a painted elevation are what people actually notice.
  • Colour matters more than size on a visible facade. Match the base to the band rather than the wall behind it.
  • Keep the line continuous. A run that stops and starts looks worse than one that runs the full band, and birds use the gaps anyway.

Working Out the Quantity

A facade is one of the few jobs that can be quantified without going up. Take the elevation drawing, or a straight-on photograph of each face, and measure:

  1. Building width × the number of horizontal bands = band metres.
  2. Sill width × the number of windows = sill metres.
  3. Parapet and cornice perimeter, including returns.
  4. Band depth for each — anything over roughly 200 mm needs two rows, which doubles those metres.
  5. Which elevations actually need doing. Faces overlooking open ground or a road are used far more than a face against the neighbouring building.

Send those numbers and the quantity can be worked out before anyone goes up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to protect the whole facade?

No. Only the projecting horizontal bands are usable — sills, string courses, sunshades, cornices, parapet. Plain wall cannot be perched on. The area needing protection is a small fraction of the elevation.

Where should I start if I cannot afford all of it?

At the top. Parapet and cornice first. Protecting the lowest bands while the highest are still in use just moves the birds up and re-fouls what you cleaned.

Will spikes spoil the look of the building?

Far less than the streaking does. Fitted on a horizontal band and seen from the ground, they read as a thin line. Matching the base colour to the band matters more than the size.

How is a facade job priced?

Material by running metre, but access usually dominates — scaffold, cradle or rope. Doing it while access is already up for painting is the single biggest saving available.

Can you quote from photographs?

Yes. A straight-on photograph of each elevation, plus the building width and the band depths, is enough to work out the quantity before anyone goes up.

Our building is heritage. Can anything be fitted?

Usually, but the fixing method and appearance both need care, and there may be consents involved. Send photographs of the specific mouldings and we will tell you what suits — see also decorative mouldings.

Do you supply for a whole society elevation?

Yes, including phased supply where the committee is doing one face at a time. Give us the elevation dimensions and band depths.
