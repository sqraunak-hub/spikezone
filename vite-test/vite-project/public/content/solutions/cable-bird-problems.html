Cables are the classic bird perch and the reason is simple: a wire is a clear, elevated, predator-free line with a view in both directions. Pigeons, mynas and crows will space themselves evenly along it and stay for hours.

Everything they do lands in a straight line beneath — on parked vehicles, an entrance, a walkway, a compound, a machine, a shopfront. The line of droppings on the ground is usually what brings people to this page, not the birds.

Why You Cannot Simply Spike a Cable

Three reasons, and the third is the important one

  • It moves. A suspended cable sways and sags with wind and temperature. Nothing bonded to it stays bonded.
  • There is no flat surface. A spike base needs something to sit against. A round cable under tension is the opposite of that.
  • It may be live, and it may not be yours. An overhead power or telecom line belongs to the utility. Attaching anything to it — or working near it — is their business, not a supplier's and not yours.

If the line carrying the birds is a utility cable, the correct step is to report it to the utility, not to buy a product. Anyone offering to fit something to a live overhead line should be declined.

What Actually Works

1. Protect What is Underneath

This is the honest answer in most cases. You cannot stop birds using a public overhead line, but you can stop the consequence landing where it matters — a net or a covering over the parking bay, the entrance, the seating area or the machine.

2. Remove the Perch if it is Yours

Disused stay wires, old cable TV runs, abandoned lines and washing lines strung across a terrace are perches you own. Taking them down removes the problem entirely and costs nothing.

3. Move the Cable into Tray or Conduit

Internal plant cabling slung between points can be re-routed into cable tray, which is a surface that can be protected. This is a permanent fix rather than a mitigation.

4. Protect the Landing Points at Each End

Birds using a cable also use the pole top, the bracket, the wall entry and the parapet at each end. Those are fixable surfaces, and taking them away makes the run less attractive even if the wire itself stays.

5. Report it, if it is a Utility Line

Sagging, low or redundant lines are worth reporting on their own merits. A tensioned or removed line is a permanent solution that costs you nothing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can bird spikes be fitted on overhead cables?

No. A cable moves, sags and offers no flat surface for a base, and an overhead line is frequently live and owned by a utility. Protect what is below instead.

How do I stop birds sitting on the wire above my parking?

You generally cannot stop them using the wire, but you can stop the droppings reaching the cars. A net over the parking bay is the standard answer.

The cable belongs to the electricity company. What are my options?

Report it to them, especially if the line is sagging or redundant. Beyond that, protect the area underneath. Do not attach anything to a utility line and do not let anybody else do so on your behalf.

What about the old cable TV wires nobody uses?

Those are worth chasing. A disused line is a perch with no purpose, and having it removed solves the problem permanently at no cost.

Can I move my cables into a tray instead?

For internal plant cabling, yes, and it is the best outcome — a tray is a surface that can be protected, unlike a slung cable. See cable trays.

Will spikes on the pole top help?

Somewhat. Birds using a run also use the pole tops, brackets and entry points at each end, and those are fixable. It reduces the attraction without removing the wire.

How is overhead netting quoted?

By the area to be covered and the span between supports. Send the dimensions of the parking bay or walkway and what is available to fix to at the edges.
