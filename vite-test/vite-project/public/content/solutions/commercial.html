The Constraints That Only Apply Here

The Work Has to Be Invisible

A hotel cannot have a cradle outside guest windows at 11am, and a showroom cannot have scaffold across its frontage on a Saturday. Commercial jobs get done at night, on a Sunday, or during a planned shutdown. That is a scheduling problem more than a technical one, and it needs to be agreed before anybody quotes.

Nothing May Fall

Above a public area, fixing method matters more than product

Over a footpath, an entrance or a forecourt, anything that comes loose lands on somebody. Adhesive alone on a high, exposed, sun-baked surface is not good enough in that position — mechanical fixing, or adhesive plus a mechanical restraint, is. Ask any supplier how the strip is secured above a public area, and be unconvinced by a vague answer.

It Has to Look Deliberate

On a visible frontage, an installation that looks improvised reads worse than the droppings did. Continuous runs rather than patches, matched base colour, and lines that follow the architecture. Details that do not matter on a warehouse matter a great deal on a hotel.

Surfaces Commercial Buildings Have That Others Do Not

  • Signage and fascia tops. A box sign has a flat top at exactly the right height, right above the entrance.
  • Awnings and canopies. The underside is sheltered and warm — a nesting site, and directly over the door.
  • Hoardings and their support frames. Large flat surfaces, high, with excellent sightlines.
  • Atrium and skylight structures. Internal steel, sometimes with birds that got in and stayed — see girders.
  • Glass canopies. Droppings are immediately visible from below and cleaning needs access every time.
  • Decorative lighting and cove details. See cove areas.

Buying It Properly

Commercial procurement asks different questions from a homeowner, and they are fair ones.

  • Quantity from drawings. Send elevations and we will work out running metres before anyone arranges access.
  • Phase it by elevation where the budget runs across quarters — but do the top bands first, or the lower ones re-foul.
  • Tie it to the repainting cycle. Access is the expensive part, and if a cradle is already up the marginal cost is close to material only.
  • Specify once for a chain. Multiple branches with the same frontage should be one specification, not a decision per site.
  • Keep spare stock on site where a facility team does its own maintenance — a metre of matching strip is cheap insurance against a patchy repair later.

Before Clearing Anything

Active nests

Most wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972. If there are eggs or chicks — and on a sheltered canopy or cornice there very often are — wait until the young have fledged, then clear and protect immediately. This is worth planning into the schedule rather than discovering on the night of the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should a commercial building start?

The entrance canopy and the signage above the door. Droppings landing on customers is a different order of problem from droppings on a terrace, and it is usually a small quantity to fix.

Will it look bad on our frontage?

Fitted on horizontal bands and seen from the ground, spikes read as a thin line. The vertical streaking down a painted facade is far more visible — that is what guests notice.

Can the work be done outside business hours?

It normally is. Nights, Sundays or a planned shutdown. Agree the window before quoting, because it affects the access arrangement more than the material.

Is anything at risk of falling onto the footpath?

Not if it is fixed properly. Above a public area, adhesive alone on an exposed sun-baked surface is not sufficient — mechanical fixing or a mechanical restraint is. Ask any supplier this question directly.

We have twenty branches. Can this be one specification?

Yes, and it should be. Send one representative elevation and we will specify it so every branch gets the same product and the same quantity logic.

Do you supply on an AMC or a facility contract?

We supply the products in bulk, including spares held on site for facility teams to make matching repairs later.

Can you quote without visiting?

Yes. Elevation drawings or straight-on photographs of each face, plus band depths, are enough to work out running metres.
