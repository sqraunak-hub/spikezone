What Is an Anti Bird Net?

An anti bird net is a mesh stretched tight across an opening and anchored to fixing points around its edge. Unlike spikes, which make a surface uncomfortable, a net physically excludes birds from a space. Nothing entangles or injures the bird — a properly tensioned net is a barrier, not a trap.

That distinction matters for choosing the right product. Spikes protect a surface. A net closes a volume. If pigeons are landing on your railing, spikes are cheaper and less visible. If they are flying into the balcony and nesting behind the AC unit, no amount of spiking on the railing will stop them.

A slack net is worse than none

This is the single biggest failure we see. A loose net sags, birds land on it, push through the gap at the edge, and then get caught between the net and the wall. Tension and proper edge fixing are not optional details — they are the installation.

Key Features

Pigeon exclusion needs a different aperture from sparrow exclusion. Get this wrong and small birds simply pass through a net that cost you a weekend.

UV-stabilised

Netting exposed to Indian sun degrades quickly if it is not UV-treated. Ours is specified for continuous outdoor exposure.

Made to measure

Cut to the opening rather than folded to fit. Folds and overlaps are exactly where birds find their way in.

Proper edge fixing

Anchors, wire rope and turnbuckles so the perimeter is closed and the mesh can be re-tensioned later if it relaxes.

Low visual impact

Humane by design

A tight net excludes birds cleanly. It is the slack ones that cause entanglement — which is why tension is the whole job.

Choosing the Right Net

Three decisions determine whether a net works. None of them is the price.

Mesh size

Chosen by the bird being excluded. A mesh that stops pigeons will not stop sparrows, and a mesh fine enough for sparrows carries more wind load and needs stronger fixing. Tell us which bird is the problem before anything else.

Fixing method

The perimeter is where nets fail. Concrete, brickwork, steel truss and glazing each need a different anchor. Rented balconies may need a non-drilling approach, which changes the design.

Tension and access

The net must be pulled tight and stay tight, which means turnbuckles or a wire rope perimeter rather than tying it off. High or awkward openings also need an access plan before anyone quotes a price.

How Netting Is Installed

  1. Survey the opening. Measure height and width, note the fixing surface on all four sides, and identify the access needed.
  2. Confirm the bird. The species decides the mesh size, and the mesh size decides the wind load and the fixings.
  3. Clear existing nests. Never close a volume with birds or nests still inside it — check before the net goes up.
  4. Fit the perimeter. Anchors and wire rope around the full edge, with no unsupported spans.
  5. Hang and tension. Attach the mesh and tension it evenly so there is no sag anywhere in the panel.
  6. Close the edges. Walk the entire perimeter looking for gaps at corners and against uneven surfaces — this is where birds get in.

Netting is not always the DIY option

A ground-floor balcony is straightforward. A shaft, a truss bay or anything needing height access is not — the fixing and tensioning are what make it work, and both are harder than they look. Send us the dimensions and we will tell you honestly which category your job is in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is anti bird netting safe for birds?

A correctly tensioned net excludes birds cleanly and safely. The risk comes from slack netting, where birds can get caught between the mesh and the wall — which is why tension and edge closure matter so much.

What mesh size do I need?

It depends on the bird. Pigeon exclusion uses a larger aperture than sparrow exclusion. Tell us which bird is causing the problem and we will specify it.

Will a net spoil my balcony view?

A properly tensioned net is far less intrusive than most people expect — it reads as a faint line rather than a curtain. If appearance is the priority and birds only perch on the railing, spikes may suit better.

Can I install netting myself?

A small, accessible balcony can be a DIY job. Shafts, truss bays and anything needing height access are not — the tensioning and perimeter fixing are the difficult parts and are also the parts that make it work.

How long does netting last?

UV-stabilised mesh that is correctly tensioned lasts many years outdoors. Most failures are fixing failures, not mesh failures.

Can netting be removed later?

Yes. The mesh comes down and the anchors can be removed, which is why it suits rented and heritage buildings where permanent changes are restricted.

Do I need society permission?

Usually yes, because netting changes the look of the elevation. Societies generally approve it more readily when the whole face is done to one specification rather than flat by flat.
