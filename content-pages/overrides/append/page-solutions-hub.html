Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know which surface is actually the problem?

Follow the droppings. A line of droppings along the ground means the birds are sitting on a horizontal edge directly above it. A scattered patch usually means they are flying through rather than perching.

Should I start from the surface or the product?

The surface, always. Its width and shape decide the product, the row count and the fixing method. Choosing a product first is how people end up with a wide strip overhanging a three-inch sill.

What if my building has several problem areas?

Treat them together and work top down — parapet first, then chajjas and sills, then railings and AC units. Doing one surface at a time just moves the birds to the next untreated edge.

How is any of this priced?

On running length — the total metres of edge to be treated — plus fixings. Surface width matters too, because anything over about four inches needs two rows.

Are spikes and nets legal to fit?

Yes. Both are passive, non-lethal deterrents and are permitted on residential and commercial buildings across India. Netting usually needs society approval because it changes the elevation; spikes usually do not.

Which surfaces are DIY and which are not?

Balconies, sills and AC units are straightforward DIY. Parapet edges, trusses, domes and anything at height need proper access and usually a contractor.

How long before the birds actually go?

Two to three weeks for an established roost. They will keep testing the spot before giving up, and removing the treatment early is the one thing that guarantees they come back.

What if I still cannot identify the surface?

Send us photographs of where the droppings are and where you have seen the birds sitting. We will tell you which surface is the problem and what it needs.
