What Makes Sills Different

A window sill is the narrowest surface birds routinely use, and that narrowness is the whole problem. A wide-base pigeon spike strip fitted to a three-inch sill hangs over the edge, looks wrong from the street and gives the adhesive less than half the contact area it needs. The strip comes loose within a season.

The second difference is quantity shape. A balcony is one long run. A flat is eight short runs in different rooms, on different elevations, some reachable and some not. Ordering "a strip" solves one window and leaves seven.

Count them all before ordering

Walk every room and count the sills, including the ones behind the AC unit and in the stairwell. Birds relocate to the nearest untreated sill within days, and a second order costs you shipping twice.

How to Measure a Flat

  1. Count the windows in every room, including kitchen, bathroom and stairwell.
  2. Measure each sill length in feet — most are 3 to 5 ft.
  3. Measure the depth. Under 4 in is one row; 4 to 8 in is two.
  4. Note the chajja above each window if there is one — it is a separate surface and often the real perch.
  5. Add 10% for cutting waste, then round up to whole strips.

Fitting Sills

  1. Clean thoroughly. Sills collect droppings in the corner against the frame — that corner is exactly where adhesive needs to bond.
  2. Cut to the exact length. Do not leave a two-inch gap at the frame; that is the spot the bird will use.
  3. Set the strip back slightly from the front edge so it is not visible from below and the adhesive gets full contact.
  4. Work from inside where possible. Most sills can be reached through an open window without going outside.
  5. Treat the chajja too if there is one. It is often the real perch and the sill is just where the droppings land.

Do not lean out of upper-floor windows

If a sill cannot be reached safely from inside, it is a job for someone with proper access. No sill is worth a fall from the fourth floor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which product suits a narrow sill?

A narrow-base single-row strip. Transparent polycarbonate is the most popular because it is invisible from inside the room.

My sill is only three inches deep. Will spikes fit?

Yes — that is exactly what the narrow-base products are for. Avoid wide pigeon strips, which will overhang.

Do I need to treat every window?

In practice yes. Birds move to the nearest untreated sill, so doing one or two just relocates the problem.

Will it look bad from the street?

Not if the strip is set slightly back from the front edge. Transparent spikes are effectively invisible.

Adhesive or screws?

Adhesive on clean stone, tile or concrete. Screws only where the surface is old and flaking — and not at all on a rented flat.

What about the chajja above the window?

Treat it at the same time. It is frequently the actual perch, with the sill simply catching what falls.
