What the Job Actually Involves

A balcony bird job is three surfaces, not one. Pigeons use the railing to perch, the sill or chajja above it to shelter, and the top of the AC outdoor unit to nest. Treat only the railing and within a week they are on the sill eighteen inches higher, and nothing has been solved.

This is the single reason most balcony installs are judged a failure. The product worked exactly as intended on the surface it was fitted to. The scope was wrong.

Walk the balcony before you order

Stand in it and look for every flat edge above knee height — railing top, sill, chajja underside lip, AC unit, grill cross-members, even the top of a storage cupboard. Those are the surfaces. Measure all of them.

How a Balcony Is Measured

  1. Railing top. Measure the full length in feet, including the returns at each end where it meets the wall.
  2. Sill or chajja. Usually the same length as the railing. Measure the depth too — over four inches needs two rows.
  3. AC outdoor unit. Measure the top surface perimeter. Under a metre on most domestic units.
  4. Add 10%. Corners, overlaps and the offcut you will need to close the last few inches of a run.
  5. Check the widths. Anything wider than about four inches takes two parallel rows, which doubles the quantity for that surface.

Spikes or Netting for a Balcony?

Watch what the birds do for a day. It answers the question completely.

  • They perch on the railing and leave. Spikes. Cheaper, invisible, no society permission, DIY in an hour.
  • They fly in and nest behind the AC unit or in a corner. Netting. The problem is the open volume, and no amount of spiking the railing will close it.

Read more on anti bird nets if it is the second one. Netting on a balcony usually needs society approval because it changes the elevation.

The Approval Step

In most societies, spikes are treated as a private fixture and need no permission — they are barely visible from the ground. Netting is different: it changes how the building face looks, and committees notice.

If you are netting, two things make approval far easier. First, ask before you fit, not after. Second, propose a single specification for the whole face rather than flat by flat, because a mixed elevation is exactly what committees refuse. Several societies have gone from refusal to standardising on one spec once it was framed that way.

Installing It Yourself

  1. Clear the nest and wash down. Remove nesting material, wash off the droppings, let it dry. Adhesive will not bond otherwise, and the smell brings the birds back.
  2. Dry-lay the strips. Lay them along each surface and cut to length with a hacksaw between pins, so every run finishes flush at the corner.
  3. Fix the railing first. Continuous bead of silicone along the base, press down firmly, hold for a few seconds.
  4. Then the chajja or sill. Two rows if it is wider than four inches — one at the front edge, one at the back.
  5. Then the AC unit. Adhesive only, never screws, and keep the airflow vents clear.
  6. Sight along each run at eye level and fill any gap with an offcut.

Give it two to three weeks

A pair that has nested on your balcony for years will keep coming back to check. That is normal and does not mean it failed. Removing the spikes early is the one thing that guarantees they return.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will spikes stop pigeons already nesting on my balcony?

Yes, provided you clear the nest and wash the area before fitting. Expect two to three weeks of the birds testing the spot before they relocate.

Do I need society permission?

For spikes, usually not — they are a private fixture and barely visible. For netting, usually yes, because it changes the building elevation.

Can I fit them on a rented balcony?

Yes. Use silicone adhesive rather than screws so nothing is drilled. It scrapes off cleanly if you move out.

How many strips do I need?

Add up the railing, the sill or chajja above it and the AC unit, then add 10%. A typical flat comes to about 40 running feet.

Will it spoil my balcony view?

Barely. Steel strips read as a thin line, and transparent polycarbonate is effectively invisible from inside the room.

Is it safe with children and pets around?

Yes. The tips are blunt. If you want the softest option, the ABS cone spikes are designed exactly for this.

What about the AC outdoor unit?

Treat it at the same time — it is one of the most common nesting spots. Adhesive only, and keep the vents clear.

How long does it take?

About an hour for a standard balcony, most of which is cleaning and dry-laying rather than fixing.
