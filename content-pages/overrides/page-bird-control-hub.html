What Is Bird Control?

Bird control means stopping birds from roosting, nesting and fouling on a building — without harming them. Everything SpikeZone makes is a passive deterrent: it changes the surface so the bird chooses to go elsewhere. There is no shock, no poison and no trap in any of it.

The problem is almost always specific. A pair of pigeons nests on one AC unit. Crows roost along one parapet. Sparrows get into one shaft. Because it is specific, the fix is specific too — and getting the product right matters more than getting the brand right.

The question that decides everything

Are you protecting a surface the birds land on, or closing an opening they fly through? Spikes do the first. Nets do the second. Almost every wrong purchase we hear about comes from answering this the other way round.

Bird Spikes or Pigeon Spikes?

They are the same technology at two scales, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. The difference is the surface, not the bird.

  • Bird spikes have a narrow base for surfaces under roughly four inches — sills, railing tops, pillar capitals, mouldings. Small orders, cut to size.
  • Pigeon spikes are about coverage — wider ledges, longer runs, full balconies. Row count and total metres are what decide the job.

If your surface is wider than about four inches, one row will not do it. Birds simply land behind the strip.

Installation Basics

All our spike products are made for DIY fitting and ship with an illustrated guide in Hindi and English. Four rules cover most of it:

  1. Treat every surface at once. Spike the railing but leave the sill above it and the birds just move up.
  2. Clean first. Remove nests and wash off droppings — adhesive will not bond to a soiled surface, and birds navigate back to the smell.
  3. Leave no gaps. Measure the full run, add about 10% for corners, and fill the last few inches with an offcut.
  4. Wait it out. Established birds test the spot for two to three weeks. Removing the spikes early undoes the whole job.

Netting is the exception

A small accessible balcony can be a DIY net job. A shaft, a truss bay or anything at height is not — tensioning and perimeter fixing are what make a net work, and a slack net is worse than no net at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are your products harmful to birds?

No. Everything we make is a passive deterrent — it makes a surface uncomfortable or closes an opening. Nothing traps, shocks or poisons the bird.

Which product do I need?

If birds land on a surface, use spikes. If they fly into a space, use a net. If it is monkeys rather than birds, you need the heavy-gauge product with mechanical fixing.

Can I install it myself?

All the spike products, yes — most customers finish a balcony in under an hour. Netting is DIY only on small accessible openings.

Do you deliver across India?

Yes, we ship pan-India, with free shipping on orders above ₹999.

Do you offer bulk pricing?

Yes. Societies, builders and industrial buyers get project rates quoted against total running length. Send us the measurements.

How long do the products last?

SS-304 stainless typically lasts well over a decade outdoors. Polycarbonate and ABS last several years in direct sun. Netting depends far more on the fixing than on the mesh.

Will spikes work on my AC outdoor unit?

Yes, and it is one of our most common applications. Use adhesive rather than screws so the casing is not pierced, and keep the vents clear.

Do I need society permission?

For spikes, usually not — they are barely visible. For netting, usually yes, because it changes the elevation. Societies approve netting more readily when a whole face is done to one specification.
