Why AC Units Attract Nesting

An outdoor unit gives a bird everything it wants in one place: a flat top surface at height, warmth from the compressor, shelter from rain under the overhang or the bracket, and almost no human disturbance because nobody goes near it for months at a time.

The cost is not just mess. Nesting material pulled into the fan grille restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder; droppings on the coil fins accelerate corrosion; and a nest wedged behind the unit is genuinely unpleasant to clear once it is established.

Never drill the casing

Screwing spikes into an outdoor unit risks the refrigerant lines and the electricals inside, and will not be looked on kindly by anyone assessing a warranty claim. Silicone adhesive holds a spike strip perfectly well on a clean metal top.

Where the Birds Actually Sit

Three places, and they need different treatment:

  • On top of the unit. The most common. Spike the top surface perimeter.
  • On the mounting bracket, under the unit. Sheltered from rain, so often preferred in monsoon. Spike the bracket arms.
  • In the gap between the unit and the wall. If the gap is large enough to nest in, it needs closing rather than spiking.

Look for where the droppings are before deciding. Droppings on the unit mean they are sitting above it; droppings on the ground directly below with a clean top usually means they are under it.

How It Is Measured

  1. Measure the top surface perimeter — length plus depth, doubled. A typical 1.5 ton unit is around 0.8 to 1 m.
  2. Add the bracket arms if birds use the underside — usually two arms of about 0.5 m each.
  3. Count the units. A three-bedroom flat often has three; a society elevation may have sixty.
  4. Check the wall above. If there is a chajja or sill immediately over the unit, treat it at the same time.

Fitting It

  1. Switch the unit off at the isolator before working on or near it.
  2. Clear the nest and wash the top. Degrease if needed — adhesive will not bond to an oily, dusty casing.
  3. Dry-lay and cut the strip to the exact top perimeter, mitring the corners so there is no gap.
  4. Run a continuous silicone bead and press the strip down firmly. Leave it undisturbed to cure.
  5. Keep the fan grille and coil face completely clear. Nothing goes across the airflow path.
  6. Treat the bracket with offcuts if the underside is in use.

Do it at the annual service

The technician already has the unit cleaned, the power isolated and a ladder in place. Adding the spikes then takes ten minutes and avoids a second visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fit spikes on my AC outdoor unit?

Yes, and it is one of our most common applications. Use silicone adhesive on the top surface and keep the vents clear.

Will it affect cooling performance?

No, provided nothing covers the fan grille or the coil face. Spikes go on the top perimeter only.

Can I screw them into the unit?

No. Never drill an outdoor unit casing — refrigerant lines and electricals sit directly behind it. Adhesive is sufficient.

How much do I need per unit?

Roughly 0.8 to 1 metre for the top perimeter of a domestic split unit, plus about a metre if you are also treating the bracket.

Birds sit under my unit, not on it. What then?

Treat the bracket arms with offcuts, and close the unit-to-wall gap if it is large enough to nest in.

When is the best time to do it?

During the annual service, when the unit is already cleaned and isolated.

Will a service technician remove them?

Adhesive-fixed strips on the top perimeter do not obstruct servicing. Mention them so nobody prises them off unnecessarily.
